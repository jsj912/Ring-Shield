## Readings (identical for Q1, Q2, Q6; exhaustive)

Let Δ be the paired mean, [lo, hi] its unadjusted 95 % interval, p_H the Holm-adjusted two-sided p,
and AMP the variant's added-member precision (mean of seeds).

1. **IMPROVES** — p_H < 0.05, Δ > 0 and AMP ≥ 0.85.
2. **REACH AT A PRECISION COST** — p_H < 0.05, Δ > 0 and AMP < 0.85. Never described as an improvement.
3. **WORSE** — p_H < 0.05 and Δ ≤ 0.
4. **NO DETECTABLE EFFECT** — p_H ≥ 0.05 and [lo, hi] ⊂ [−0.02, +0.02].
5. **INCONCLUSIVE** — p_H ≥ 0.05 and [lo, hi] not inside [−0.02, +0.02].

## Q1 — score saturation

**Question.** Do ties in `score_raw` decide which edges become seeds at 0.1 %?

**Pre-test measure (per seed, validation and test):** the number of edges tied at the maximum
score; the threshold q of the strict rule; the number of edges with score exactly q (excluded by
`s > q`); and the share of the budget decided by a tie = (edges with score = q) / (budget N =
⌈0.001 · n_edges⌉ per the shipped `threshold_for_pct`). **Stop rule:** if that share is < 1 % on every
seed in both splits, Q1 reports the counts and stops, with no test run and no family membership.

**Variant (only if the stop rule does not fire).** Seeds are selected on `score_raw` already, and
no logit is stored (scores files hold `score_raw`, `score_calibrated` only). The tie-breaker is therefore the
brief's fallback: rank by (`score_raw`, then the mean `score_raw` of the other edges of the edge's
two accounts in the same split, then edge_id ascending), and take exactly the top N edges, where
N = the shipped seed count for that seed and split. Seed-only and full are rerun with the re-ranked
seeds through the shipped route. No validation grid. Hypothesis: Δ ≈ 0; the part exists to close
the tie-handling item of the reporting checklist.

## Q2 — account-level seeding

**Rule.** Account score a(v) from the raw scores of v's incident edges in the split: variant
**Q2-max** = maximum; variant **Q2-top3** = mean of the three highest (mean of all if fewer than three).
Rank accounts by a(v) descending (ties: account id ascending). Walk the ranking; each account
contributes its incident edges not yet chosen, in descending `score_raw`, at most three; stop as
soon as the chosen set reaches N edges, where N = the shipped global-rule seed count for that seed and
split; the last account contributes only as many edges as needed, so the total equals N exactly.

**How it differs from 11c (`evaluation/local_budget_seeds.py`).** 11c keeps the *edge* ranking. It moves
every edge that is the top edge of at least one endpoint ("local maximum") above every other edge,
then applies the global strict cut. So every 11c seed is ranked by its own score, and an account
holds at most the edges that are its own maximum or its neighbour's. Q2 ranks *accounts*, and a top
account brings up to three of its edges even when none is a local maximum of the other endpoint.
Under Q2-top3, an account with several high-scoring edges outranks one with a single higher edge,
which 11c cannot express. Q2 therefore concentrates the budget on fewer, multi-edge accounts (the
opposite direction from 11c's spreading, which lost 13.4 touched rings in B14). It can behave
differently from both global and 11c, so it is run.

**Variant choice.** One of Q2-max and Q2-top3 is chosen per the full pipeline's mean validation
ring-F1 over the five seeds (ties → Q2-max). Only the chosen variant is run on test and enters the
family. The other variant's validation numbers are reported.

**Hypothesis.** Q0 puts 11.0 test rings per seed in bucket b (no seed touches them) and 7.8 in c1
with no prediction. Account-level seeding moves seeds and may reach some of them. B14 predicts that
reallocation at a fixed budget gains little or loses.

## Q6 — undersized-group rescue (added after Q0)

**Target.** A *group* is a connected component of the shipped included edges (seeds + edges the
shipped expansion added at p_min 0.7) after the shipped expansion. Q6 acts **only on groups with
fewer than three accounts**, which the shipped pipeline discards. Groups with ≥ 3 accounts, and
everything downstream of them (partition, rings), are left exactly as shipped. The brief's second
clause ("groups the validation decomposition marks as clean but too small") **is not applied**:
identifying such a group needs truth labels, which are unavailable on test. This deviation is the
analyst's and is reported.

**Shipped parameters relaxed** (shipped values from `experiments/R3_expansion/runs/proposed/seed*/config.yaml`
and `ringshield/aggregators/expand.py`): acceptance probability `p_min` = **0.7** (val-chosen from
{0.3, 0.5, 0.7}, all five seeds); `max_hops` = **2** (fixed; `candidates.py` asserts 1–2). Unchanged:
Δt 24 h, `MAX_ITER` 200, `MAX_EVENTS_PER_QUERY` 500, closure stop, seed window.

**Variants and validation grid (8 cells, fixed now):**
- shipped (no rescue; validation may choose it);
- (i) lower p_min ∈ {0.5, 0.3, 0.1}, hops 2;
- (ii) one extra hop: hops 3, p_min 0.7;
- (iii) both: hops 3, p_min ∈ {0.5, 0.3, 0.1}.
Hop 3 is implemented in `evaluation/q6_rescue.py` (not in `ringshield/`, which is read-only here).
It applies the hop-2 rule once more from each hop-2 candidate's far endpoint, within Δt of that
candidate, under the same event cap. The policy was trained on hops 1–2 only, so hop-3 rows are
scored with `hop` = 3 and the policy extrapolates. That is stated as a caveat on any hop-3 result.

**Rescue rule.** Each undersized group is re-expanded from its own seeds with the cell's
(p_min, hops). Rescued edges that touch an account of a shipped ≥ 3-account group are dropped, so
shipped groups are not altered. Each rescued component with ≥ 3 accounts becomes one ring (no partition).

**Selection.** Per detector seed, the cell with the highest validation ring-F1 (full + rescued
rings, `score_external.py --split val --window val`). Ties go to the cell closest to shipped: higher
p_min, then fewer hops. Choices are written to `experiments/Q6_rescue/choices.json` before any test
scoring.

**Hypothesis and prediction.** Q6 targets bucket c1 with no prediction (7.8 per seed on test). By
FINDINGS B13/B16, STACK's missing members are disconnected in the window and in history, so **no
STACK gain is expected**. Results are reported per pattern with STACK shown separately. A STACK gain
would contradict B13 and would be checked for leakage before being reported.
